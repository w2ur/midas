# Midas — Methodology & Limitations

*Pre-registered evaluation methodology. This document states what Midas measures, how, and — deliberately, up front — everything that is wrong or unproven about it. It is versioned in git, and every substantive change is logged, dated, in the changelog at the end of this document — the public record of what was claimed when. (The full commit history lives in the live-desk repository, which is not public today; see "The engine is open source" below.)*

Last substantive update: 2026-08-07.

---

## What Midas is

Midas is a public experiment: ten Claude trading personas, each started with €10,000 of **paper** money on **2026-04-17**, evaluate the market and journal their reasoning autonomously every weekday with no human in the loop — authoring an order only when conviction clears the bar, which most agents do on a minority of sessions (see the live cadence figures below). An eleventh agent, the Oracle, narrates but does not trade. Every order, the reasoning behind it, every fill, and every portfolio snapshot is committed to a git repository and rendered on this site. The engine that runs the desk is open source (MIT); the full live-desk repository — the complete data ledger — is not public today. The site at `midas.revah.paris` renders entirely from those committed artifacts.

This is an experiment in *process and transparency*, not a demonstration of trading skill. Read the limitations section before drawing any conclusion from a number on the leaderboard.

## What is measured

For each agent we track the EUR mark-to-market value of its portfolio over time, and its return since inception. Returns convert all positions to EUR (the operator is a French tax resident); a USD-denominated holding therefore carries a currency-translation effect — see *Known distortions* below.

## The controls (this is the part most projects skip)

A return number in isolation is meaningless. Every agent is graded against **two controls**, both rendered publicly on its dossier:

1. **Passive benchmark** — a buy-and-hold of the agent's natural reference (e.g. SPY-equivalent for a US-equity agent, BTC-EUR for the crypto agent, a flat EUR-cash line for the FX agent). Beating this is the minimum bar for "the trading added anything."
2. **Coin-flip phantom** — a portfolio that made *random* trades of the same cadence and sizing in the same universe. Beating this is the minimum bar for "the decisions were better than chance."

A global **MSCI World** reference line appears on the leaderboards as a market-wide anchor.

An agent that beats its benchmark but loses to its own coin-flip has demonstrated nothing but luck. We show both deltas next to every return so that this cannot be hidden.

## Costs

From **2026-06-13**, paper fills carry a realistic, per-asset-class fee model (`engine/fees.py`):

- **Equities / ETFs**: ~0.05% with a €1.25 per-order floor — Interactive Brokers Ireland tiered pricing.
- **Crypto**: 0.40% (Kraken spot taker; the cheaper 0.25% maker rate is *not* assumed — we use the conservative number).
- **FX**: ~0.002% spread proxy.

Fees are deducted from cash on every fill and tighten the cash-availability check. **Historical `bt` backtests (factor research and the standalone backtester service) are gross of costs**, however: `bt`'s commission hook does not expose the ticker, so the per-asset-class fee model cannot be mapped into it faithfully without a non-trivial signature adaptation. That full wiring is deferred; until then those backtests carry an explicit `GROSS_OF_COSTS` warning in the CLI output and the backtester API `warnings` field, and their returns should be read as fee-free. **Before 2026-06-13 the paper simulation modelled zero fees.** We do not retroactively rewrite history; the pre-fix period is fenced and returns from that window are fee-free and should be read as such. A separate **after-tax shadow ledger** (`data/tax_shadow/`) estimates the French flat tax (PFU, 30%) that realized gains would incur — reporting only; it never affects the portfolios. Its crypto figure is a documented per-coin approximation of the statutory global-portfolio method (see the module docstring); it will be replaced with the exact method before any real capital is deployed.

<a id="the-noise-statement"></a>

## The noise statement

**Eight weeks is statistical noise.** With roughly 35 daily observations, the variance of returns dwarfs any plausible skill signal; distinguishing skill from luck at conventional confidence would take years of data, not weeks. **Four months in, the controls put a number on it** (measured 2026-08-14, 73 valuation points): three coin-flip portfolios drawing from near-identical European equity universes landed at **−4.4%, +1.7% and +8.1%** over the same window — a 12-point span from randomness alone. Any benchmark-relative delta inside roughly ±6pp is indistinguishable from luck at this sample size, which today is most of the board. Accordingly:

> **No claim of skill, edge, or "Claude can trade" will be made on the basis of this experiment until at least 6 months have elapsed AND each agent has accumulated ≥100 fills — and even then, only relative to the two controls above, net of fees and modelled tax.**

This threshold is pre-registered here so that a later good run cannot be retrofitted into a skill claim. The live status against that threshold — fills accumulated per agent, the binding constraint, and whether the desk is on track — is generated below from the same data as the leaderboard, not hand-typed.

## Known distortions in the current leaderboard

These are real and we surface them rather than letting a reader discover them as a "gotcha." Figures below are illustrative, drawn from the standings around 2026-06-06; the live site carries current numbers.

- **Currency translation, not skill.** The EUR weakened against the USD over the sample. Because returns are reported in EUR, every USD-denominated book received a mechanical translation boost. For the leading agent (`sharp-shooter-usd`, ~+7.8% at the time), roughly **2.35 percentage points** of the return is this currency-accounting effect, not a trading decision. *Since [2026-08-14](/methodology/changelog#leaderboard-rerank-2026-08-14) the ranking no longer reads this number: agents rank on benchmark-relative return in their own currency, and each non-EUR row states its translation leg as `fx_translation_pp`. The raw EUR column keeps the distortion and keeps this disclosure.*
- **Universe mix, not selection.** US equities outperformed European equities by ~6pp over the sample. A further ~3.5pp of that same agent's lead reflects *which market it was assigned*, not which stocks it picked. Net of both effects, the nominal leader and the second-place agent are within ~0.1pp of each other in native-currency terms — a statistical tie. *Also addressed by the [2026-08-14 re-rank](/methodology/changelog#leaderboard-rerank-2026-08-14): measuring each book against its own market's benchmark removes the assignment effect from the ranking, though not from the raw EUR column.*
- **Day-one inception artifact.** One agent (`world`) recorded a first-day mark-to-market above its €10,000 baseline, inflating its reported return by an amount unrelated to subsequent decisions (true return from actual day-1 NAV is materially lower than the headline).
- **Lost fill, `sharp-shooter-eur` (2026-05-21) — reconciled 2026-08-02.** A conditional-order sale was confirmed by the broker but its portfolio write was lost to an infrastructure bug. This was originally disclosed and *not* corrected; on 2026-08-02 that refusal was reversed, because keeping it meant publishing a book that had sold four shares out of three. The lost sale was inserted, the later sale it invalidated was removed, the corresponding inbox fill was voided rather than deleted, and the reported return moved from **+3.30% to +0.19%**. The rewrite itself went unlogged for five days. Full record in the [2026-08-07 changelog entry](#sweep-and-restatement-2026-08-07); the original incident and the reasoning that was overridden are in the [2026-07-27 entry](#lost-fill-2026-05-21).
- **European fills execute on older closes than American ones.** The vendor serves a European close late — as a NULL row the next morning, then absent, reliably populated only for the day before — so at a 20:00 UTC session the price store holds the previous day's US closes but *two-day-old* European ones. European books therefore fill at closes up to two trading days old, while every agent reads same-day inputs (the two treatment agents read same-day headlines): a structural look-ahead in the European books' favour, and a staleness cost in everyone's. Disclosed while being measured: a scheduled probe (`eu-close-probe`, since 2026-08-14) is establishing the hour a European close becomes reliably available, and the fix will be collection-side, not a restatement — no published fill moves.
- **Survivorship bias in historical factor research.** The index universes (`sp500`, `nasdaq100`, `cac40`, `dax`, `ftse100`, `stoxx-600`) resolve to their **current** constituents, committed in `data/universes/*.json`. Backtesting one of these over a historical window silently trades only the names that survived to today — the delisted losers were never in the sample. An early factor-research run against the S&P 500's current membership from 2024 reported returns inflated by roughly **194%** versus the same run on a survivorship-free universe. We cannot reconstruct point-in-time membership without a historical-constituents feed we do not have, so the mitigation is a loud `SURVIVORSHIP_BIAS` warning (`engine/survivorship.py`) emitted by the CLI backtesters and the backtester API whenever a run's start date predates the universe file's last refresh. The factor-research and Add-a-Strategy defaults were moved to `dow30` (a stable, slow-moving 30-name universe) and `etf-broad`, which do not carry this distortion.

## How execution is disciplined

- **Safety lives in the broker, not the prompt.** The paper broker enforces 19 distinct rejection/cancel reason codes (cash, shares, notional, universe, drawdown, FX-rate, order-count, trigger-expiry, agent cancellations, unresolvable quote currency, implausible fill prices and trigger levels, and more). A persona's prompt is aspirational; the broker is what actually constrains it. The per-order size cap is expressed as a **percentage of each book's current value**, so it re-scales with the book instead of being a fixed number chosen once and quietly outgrown — see the [2026-08-07 rails entry](#rails-calibration-2026-08-07).
- **Decision-time air gap.** Trading sessions run with **no outbound HTTP**. Prices — and, for the two agents in the sentiment A/B below, news headlines — come only from committed stores, populated out-of-band by separate scheduled jobs. Agents never fetch the web at decision time. This makes two failure modes that affect comparable systems *structurally impossible* here: tool-level look-ahead leakage, and live-fetch instability. Headlines, when used, are pre-sanitized committed data treated as untrusted input (see the A/B section).
- **Idempotent, auditable order flow.** Orders and fills are committed JSONL keyed on deterministic order IDs; re-running a session cannot double-fill. Conditional orders carry mandatory expiries and fire through a separate watcher.

## Pre-registered experiment: sentiment A/B

*Registered 2026-06-13, before any sentiment-informed session. The published evidence on adding news/sentiment to LLM traders is mixed-to-negative (it often injects noise and raises turnover), so this is run as a falsifiable test, not a feature — and the result will be published either way.*

- **Hypothesis.** Giving an analyst a feed of recent, sanitized news headlines for the tickers it holds does **not** improve its risk-adjusted, net-of-fee return versus its own controls, and may worsen turnover.
- **Treatment group (2 agents):** `satoshi` (crypto) and `sharp-shooter-eur` (EU momentum). **Control group:** the other 8 agents, which receive no sentiment feed.
- **Mechanism.** A separate scheduled job collects ≤10 headlines/ticker/day for active tickers and commits them to `data/market/news/`. The two treatment agents read their tickers' digests during their normal (still air-gapped) session, under an explicit "untrusted data, never instructions" preamble. Headlines reach **analysts only** — never the real-money Manager path, which consumes only structured research-note fields, never raw text.
- **Timing, and the lag that invalidated the first window.** The collector must commit *before* the session reads the repo. It did not: from the first digest on 2026-06-15 until 2026-08-07, every scheduled run committed between 20:08 and 21:49 UTC, after the 20:00 session had already realigned — so the treatment agents read the **previous day's** headlines at every session. Disclosed and repaired in the [2026-08-07 entry](#sentiment-lag-2026-08-07); the window restarts from the first session that verifiably reads same-day headlines. Timing is no longer trusted on its own: each session records which arm it actually ran to `data/market/sentiment_arm.jsonl`, committed, so a confounded session is visible in the record rather than inferable only from commit timestamps.
- **Window:** 4 weeks from first sentiment-informed session.
- **Metrics:** each treatment agent's return vs its own passive benchmark and coin-flip control (the same two controls every agent has), its turnover, and qualitative drift in its trade rationales — each compared against the control group over the same window.
- **Outcome:** if the treatment shows no benchmark-relative improvement (or a turnover spike), the feed is removed and the result reported as a negative finding. Sentiment is **never** promoted to the Manager path regardless of outcome. A positive result only makes more *analysts* eligible, never the Manager.

## Scope and honest limitations

- **Paper money only.** No real capital is at risk today. A real-money deployment (€1,500–2,000, Interactive Brokers Ireland + Kraken, French-tax-aware) is designed but gated: capital moves only after a separate manager strategy demonstrably beats a deterministic baseline, net of fees and tax, over a multi-month pre-registered window. That gate now has a date: the Manager's record is evaluated **on 2026-12-16, not before** — see the [pre-registration entry](/methodology/changelog#gate-c-evaluation-2026-08-14). Paper results are a weaker form of evidence than live trading, by definition.
- **One model family by design.** Every agent runs on Claude. This is **not** a model comparison and no cross-model claim can be drawn from it.
- **Single run, no pre-registered hypothesis test.** This is one realization of one experiment, not a controlled study with replication. Treat it as a transparent case study, not a result.
- **Small sample, short horizon.** See the noise statement.

## What you may fairly conclude today

That ten Claude agents have evaluated the market and paper-traded autonomously, every weekday since 2026-04-17 — authoring a trade only on a minority of sessions — with every decision and its reasoning committed to git and rendered on this site line by line (the engine is open source; the full live-desk repository is not public today); that each is measured against both a passive benchmark and a random-trade control shown beside its return; and that the whole thing was built and run by one person. Nothing about returns, edge, or whether "AI can trade." Those questions are deliberately deferred to the thresholds above.

**Interim observations at four months** (2026-08-14, 73 valuation points, 247 fills — observations about *this record*, not claims about skill; every figure below sits inside or near the ±6pp noise floor stated above):

- **Eight of the ten books sit at or below their own passive benchmark.** The desk's mean EUR return is +0.46% against MSCI World's +7.45%. If this experiment ends up documenting that autonomous LLM traders mostly underperform passive exposure net of fees, that is a publishable result, and the current record points that way.
- **Activity has not paid.** The most active book (40 fills, turnover ~6× its capital) landed below both its benchmark and its own coin flip; the two least active books hold the top benchmark-relative positions. Consistent with fees-times-turnover arithmetic; not evidence that patience is skill.
- **The EUR/USD twins cannot isolate anything, by construction.** Each pair differs in *universe and currency simultaneously*, so their gap never attributes to either — decomposed on 2026-08-14, the steady-eddie twins' 15.0pp headline gap was ~5.2pp market assignment, ~2.4pp currency translation, ~7.5pp residual. The twins stay, as same-persona-different-market exhibits, not as an identification strategy.
- **Two mandates produce little evidence.** The unlevered FX book has moved ±0.2% in four months against a cash benchmark of zero — spot FX majors without leverage cannot express a view at measurable scale. The commodities book tracks its gold benchmark within a point (−8.7% vs −8.0%). Both are disclosed as exhibits rather than deleted; changing a mandate mid-experiment is a methodology change and would be logged as one.

## The engine is open source

The engine that runs this experiment is public at [github.com/w2ur/midas-core](https://github.com/w2ur/midas-core) under the MIT licence. That includes the **Brain/Hands** architecture — agents author orders to an outbox on disk; a paper broker enforces 15 distinct rejection/cancel reason codes and writes fills to an inbox — the `roster.yaml` config that drives the whole cast (traders, a narrator, and an allocator, each with its own safety rails enforced in the broker rather than the prompt), and a runnable demo desk with a documented walkthrough. Anyone can clone it and stand up their own desk: the quickstart in the [midas-core README](https://github.com/w2ur/midas-core#readme) is the starting point. A fuller account of the architecture, the rails, and what is and is not public is at [/open-source](/open-source).

What is **not** public is this live desk's own repository — the full data ledger of orders, fills, and snapshots. The site renders those committed artifacts, but the repository itself is private today. So the framework is open and reproducible; the specific book you are reading here is shown, not shared.

## Methodology changelog

*Methodology changes are logged here rather than silently applied — pre-registration honesty.*

- <a id="gate-c-evaluation-2026-08-14"></a>**2026-08-14 — The real-money gate's evaluation date is pinned: 2026-12-16, six months of Manager record. Registered now so the decision cannot be made by glancing at a good week.** The Manager (the allocator whose track record gates any real-money deployment) has run since 2026-06-16; as of this entry its book reads +0.14% against its shadow baseline's −0.88% — a ~1pp edge over two months on a €2,000 book, which is nothing, and the temptation with a number like that is to keep peeking until it looks like something. So the evaluation is scheduled the way the sentiment A/B was: date fixed in advance, criteria stated before the result is known. On **2026-12-16** the Manager's record is scored against its deterministic baseline, net of fees and modelled tax, and the outcome entry must record the three things that cannot be reconstructed afterwards: **(1)** the commit SHA the evaluation window was scored at, **(2)** which ledger basis it was scored on, by name, and **(3)** confirmation that the Manager's decision prompts were rendering currency-labelled position values throughout the window. Between now and then the record is not consulted for the gating decision — interim figures may appear in ordinary reporting, but no go/no-go reading happens before the date, and an early evaluation for any reason would be its own changelog entry explaining why. Nothing about today's standings moves; this entry exists precisely so that nothing has to be trusted about the state of mind of whoever reads the December number.

- <a id="leaderboard-rerank-2026-08-14"></a>**2026-08-14 — The leaderboard now ranks on benchmark-relative return. No published number moves; what changes is which number the ranking reads.** Since day one the board ordered agents by EUR-normalised return — and since June the [Known distortions section](#known-distortions) above has disclosed that this ordering mixes three things: currency translation (EUR fell ~2% against USD over the sample, a mechanical ~+2.1pp tailwind to every USD book), market assignment (S&P 500 +8.8% vs FTSE Europe +3.6% in local terms — which market an agent was *given*, not what it picked), and whatever skill remains. Decomposed on the steady-eddie twins at this change's date: of their 15.0pp leaderboard gap, ~5.2pp is market, ~2.4pp is FX translation, ~7.5pp is residual. Disclosing a distortion every day while ranking on it anyway was the worse half of honesty; the rank now reads the number the distortion section always said was the right one. Each row carries `vs_benchmark_pp` — the book's return **in its own currency** minus its own pre-registered benchmark's return, FX-free by construction — plus `vs_coinflip_pp` (same measure against the coin-flip control) and, on non-EUR books, `fx_translation_pp`, the translation leg stated as a number instead of prose. The raw EUR return keeps its column, unchanged and still published on every row; an agent with no baseline series ranks after all measured rows rather than as best or worst. **This entry also corrects two site-side defects the change surfaced.** First, the dossier and homepage "vs benchmark" figures were computed as *EUR-normalised* return minus *book-currency* benchmark return — a difference that silently embedded the FX leg on USD books (`steady-eddie-usd` displayed +7.53pp where the currency-consistent figure is +5.17pp). The site now renders the engine's numbers and computes none of its own. Second, USD dossiers stated "Starting capital: USD 10,000"; every book in fact started as **EUR 10,000**, converted at day one for USD books (~$11,783 at EURUSD 1.178), which is the base their local returns are measured off. Effect on the standings the day it shipped: `satoshi` leads at **+6.64pp** over BTC-EUR (raw EUR return −9.4%), `steady-eddie-usd` second at **+5.17pp** over the S&P 500 — and eight of ten books sit at or below their own benchmark, which the old ordering obscured. One thing this change does *not* do: make the ranking meaningful at this sample size. Three coin-flip controls drawing from near-identical European universes span **12 points** (−4.4% to +8.1%) over the same window, so most benchmark-relative deltas are still inside noise — the [noise statement](#the-noise-statement) stands regardless of metric.

- <a id="complete-bars-2026-08-12"></a>**2026-08-12 (later) — Crypto and FX now mark at a complete daily close instead of a bar that was still forming. No published row is restated; only future marks change.** The price collector was moved from 22:30 UTC to 06:00 UTC to fix a separate defect — at 22:30 the European cash close for the day was not yet published, so the store ran one European trading day behind the American one every night for five weeks. That move exposed something the old hour had been hiding rather than avoiding. The collector asked the vendor for **today**, and while a cash market simply serves nothing for a day that has not opened, a 24/7 instrument does not: Yahoo Finance publishes a daily bar for crypto and FX the moment the UTC day begins. Measured on 2026-08-12, a same-day `BTC-USD` close is returned at **07:49 UTC**. So at 22:30 the desk was marking crypto at a bar about **22.5 hours** into its formation, and at 06:00 it would have been marking one about **6 hours** in. Either way the evening session publishes that number and `PortfolioManager.add_snapshot` freezes it, so the following morning's correction — the revision window exists precisely for this — arrives after the row it would have fixed is already immutable. The collector now asks for **yesterday**, never today. Every bar it stores is a completed daily bar on every instrument, and the published crypto and FX mark is a **final close** rather than a snapshot of a day in progress. **This is a change to how future marks are taken, not a restatement**: no snapshot, baseline point, fill, cash balance or published return has been recalculated or moved, and the historical crypto series keeps the partial values it was recorded with — the 2026-08-06 entry below already discloses that roughly half of the crypto history sits at partial closes, and that remains true and remains unrepaired. **On the scheduled path the dates do not shift**, which is worth stating because it would be an easy thing to break silently: at 06:00 the store advances to the previous day, and that evening's session publishes a row dated to the previous day — the same date the 22:30 collector produced. The 2026-08-04 session published a 2026-08-03 row and the 2026-08-05 session published a 2026-08-04 one. **The record also holds rows dated on the session's own day, and they are the other half of this entry.** The 2026-08-07 and 2026-08-10 snapshots are both same-day, and in each case a run that executed *on* that day had asked the vendor for **today** and written a still-forming crypto bar for the benchmark the snapshot date is taken from — at 01:47 UTC on 08-07, and at 20:06 UTC on 08-10, six minutes into the session itself. Ending at yesterday makes a same-day-dated row **structurally impossible** rather than merely unlikely, at whatever hour a run happens to fire.

**That is the most valuable thing this change does, and it is not hypothetical.** A snapshot date may be claimed only once — see the [2026-08-03 entry](#snapshot-overwrite-2026-08-03): a row may be replaced only by the session that wrote it, and a later session asking for the same date is refused. So a session that publishes *its own* day's date consumes the date its successor was going to use, and the successor records nothing at all. That is what happened on **2026-08-11**: the 08-10 session had claimed an 08-10-dated row, the store had not advanced past 08-10 by the time the 08-11 session read it, and **the 2026-08-11 session published no snapshot for any of the twelve books** — every book's newest published market date is still 2026-08-10. The immutability guard behaved exactly as designed; what fed it was a date that should never have existed. With the collector ending at yesterday, a session can no longer claim its own day and starve the next one. **None of this is a restatement**: the missing 2026-08-11 point is not being backfilled, and no published row moves.

- <a id="sentiment-collection-hour-2026-08-12"></a>**2026-08-12 — The sentiment A/B's treatment changed content mid-window. Nothing is mislabelled and no published number moves, but the word "treatment" now covers two different things, and the window restarts again shortly.** The news collector moved from 18:00 UTC to **09:00 UTC** (cron `0 9 * * 1-5`). It was moved for a session start that has not happened yet: the trading session is scheduled to move from 20:00 UTC to 11:00 UTC, and at 18:00 a collector would have landed *after* an 11:00 session every day and turned the treatment arm into a control arm permanently. Until that session move lands, the pairing is **09:00 collected, 20:00 read** — so for this interval the two treatment agents (`sharp-shooter-eur`, `satoshi`) receive a digest assembled **before the US cash session opens**, where an 18:00 digest covered that session in full. That is not a lag: the digest is same-day, and `step_check_sentiment_freshness` correctly records `treatment` for it. It is a change in what the treatment *is*. The [2026-08-07 entry](#sentiment-lag-2026-08-07) below restarted this window, and it is **three sessions old** — 2026-08-07, 2026-08-10 and 2026-08-11, all recorded `treatment`, all collected at 18:00. Those three sessions and everything from 2026-08-12 onward are not the same experimental condition, so they are not poolable, and the window **restarts a second time from the first session that runs at 11:00 UTC against the 09:00 collector** — the pairing the design actually calls for, a 2 h margin with the digest ahead of the session's own trading day. Recorded here rather than absorbed quietly because the arm log answers "which arm ran" and cannot answer "which treatment", and a reader comparing the two agents against the other eight is entitled to know the second question exists. No historical row is restated and no published return, snapshot or fill changes; the effect is entirely on what future sessions read.

- <a id="close-basis-2026-08-07"></a>**2026-08-07 (last) — Every price is now read on the raw close. The dividend-adjusted basis is gone, 861 published rows move — 104 agent valuations and 757 coin-flip control points — and the direction of the change flatters the agents against one control.** The [sweep entry below](#sweep-and-restatement-2026-08-07) closed by disclosing an asymmetry it had created rather than reversing it: every price reader in the engine took Yahoo's `adj_close` when a row had one, that preference had been inert before the sweep, and the sweep made it live on **8,295 of 79,936 in-window rows across 291 symbols**. The wrinkle was described there as "not a design decision anyone made deliberately". This entry makes the decision. **The basis is now the raw `close`, everywhere, and `adj_close` stays in the store as vendor data that nothing reads.**

  **Why price return is the right basis here, and not merely the simpler one.** Two reasons, and the first is the one that matters. **The paper broker never credits dividend cash.** A book holding a payer receives nothing on the ex-date — no cash appears, no share is reinvested. Valuing that position on a dividend-*reinvested* series therefore credits the book with a return it demonstrably did not receive; the money is not in its cash and never was. Price return on `close` is the only basis internally consistent with how this desk actually executes. Second, and independently: **Yahoo re-bases `adj_close` across a symbol's entire history after every payout.** A field the vendor rewrites retroactively cannot sit underneath an append-or-refuse contract — the same date would price differently on two different days, for a reason no one recorded and no session caused. That is precisely the retroactive drift the [published-data gates](#published-data-gates-2026-08-07) exist to refuse, and it was entering through a field rather than through a writer. Splits are not a counter-argument: Yahoo restates the raw `close` itself for a split, and `engine.corporate_actions` adjudicates that on the store side.

  **What moved, measured in three parts rather than one.** A recomputation shows a series' *total* movement, which here is two different things added together, and only one of them is this change. They are separated below, because restating the other one is exactly the mistake logged in the [store-units entry](#store-units-2026-08-07), where a blanket restatement flag moved eight passive benchmarks on fresher **prices** rather than on the intended basis and they had to be restored by hand.
  - **Agent snapshots — 104 rows, four books.** `steady-eddie-eur` 47, `world` 30, `sharp-shooter-eur` 25, `yolo-sapiens-eur` 2. All four are EUR books, because the affected names are dividend-paying European equities — `SAN.PA`'s adjusted close sits **5.29% below** its raw close on 2026-05-04, `ALV.DE` **4.40% below** on 2026-04-30. Every move is **upward** (an adjusted historical price sits below the raw one), largest **+1.71%** on `steady-eddie-eur`'s 2026-04-20 row, €9,801.78 → €9,969.67. **No agent's headline return changes at all** — all twelve books report the same figure to two decimals, because a bar captured today has no later dividend applied to it and its two fields are equal, so only historical rows move. What changes is the *shape* of four curves: the sweep had pulled those historical points down, which mechanically flattered the slope from those dates forward. This removes that flattery. A further **181 rows were left completely untouched** — the rows whose effective session date cannot be resolved from their own recorded cash, which this tooling refuses to guess at rather than invent a composition for.
  - **Coin-flip controls — 757 rows across seven series**, restated onto the new basis rather than left with a seam. - <a id="rails-calibration-2026-08-07"></a>**2026-08-07 (later still) — The safety rails were recalibrated mid-experiment, and the broker gained four ways to refuse an order.** A rails change is a methodology change, so it is logged here rather than applied quietly.
  **The rails could not bind.** The ten traders had run since day one with a per-order cap of **1,000,000**, a daily order limit of **100**, and a drawdown halt at **−95%** — against books of €10,000. The cap was a hundred times the entire portfolio; no agent has ever authored more than 27 orders in a day; and a halt that waits for a book to lose 95% is not a halt. Those are not limits, they are decoration, and the preceding entries describe exactly the class of error they failed to stop: a fill of €1,090 for €10.90 of Lloyds passed every one of them, because nothing in the broker had an opinion about whether a number was plausible. The cap is *relative* deliberately: a fixed cap has to be chosen against a book size and stops binding the moment the book moves, which is how the previous one survived four months unnoticed. The allocator's existing 500 / 5 / −5% is unchanged.
  **Four new refusals.** `CURRENCY_UNRESOLVED` — a ticker whose quote currency is in neither the override map nor the vendor registry, and whose exchange suffix is unrecognised, is now refused instead of silently assumed to be dollars (the fourth, latent failure mode described in the entry below). `PRICE_IMPLAUSIBLE` — a fill more than 5x, or less than a fifth of, its reference is refused; the reference is the *prior* stored close on a buy and the position's own cost on a sell. `TRIGGER_LEVEL_IMPLAUSIBLE` — a conditional order whose level is nowhere near the live price is refused at authoring time, so it never becomes an armed instruction; this is the rail for the pence-denominated stop described below, which no check in the system could previously see. `VALUATION_UNAVAILABLE` — a book that cannot be valued now halts instead of trading, where the drawdown rail previously read an unvaluable book as "no drawdown" and switched itself off.
  **The bands are loose on purpose, and the looseness was measured rather than assumed.** 5x is not a market-move detector — a stock can halve and a crypto pair can double. Replayed against the full committed ledger, all **216** fills sit between **0.91x and 1.07x** of their prior close, and all **68** live pending orders sit within **[0.77, 2.06]** of their ticker's price; the pence-stop incident sits at **95.7**. Recomputing would therefore have folded a silent FX-rate restatement of every touched row into what must be a currency-only correction, and left the corrected rows anchored to a different rate source than the untouched ones. So each affected notional is **scaled** by `(native_price / recorded_price) × (rate_new / rate_old)`, both rates taken from the same source on the same date: the broker's own executed rate stays the anchor, and only the currency identification changes. For `LLOY.L`, where both readings are sterling and only the unit was wrong, the factor collapses to exactly 1/100 — the right answer by inspection. Fees were recomputed only where a fee was actually charged; a fill recorded with `fees: 0.0` predates `engine/fees.py` and does not acquire a retroactive commission. Every mutated record — 24 trades, 24 inbox fills, and one position's cost basis — keeps its prior values under `originally_recorded` with a `reconciliation_note`, the same convention as the 2026-08-02 reconciliation: the record still shows what the broker actually did.
